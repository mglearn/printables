Text-specific discussion questions (open — more than one answer)

  • Predict: Dana is yanked into the past whenever Rufus is in danger — what will saving him force her to become part of?
  • Question: Why might Butler make Dana’s survival depend on protecting a future slaveholder?
  • Clarify: What does Dana’s injury on returning to 1976 suggest about the past’s grip on the present?
  • Summarize: Trace how Dana’s relationship to Rufus changes across her trips, without opinion.
  • Interpret the theme: How do the time shifts reveal a theme about slavery’s legacy and power? Use two details (paraphrase).
Optional Jigsaw lenses (p. 4): Character (Dana and Rufus) · Structure (the time-travel frame) · Theme (the power and legacy of slavery) · Setting (1976 vs. antebellum Maryland) · Point of view (Dana’s first-person witness).

Sample 3–2–1 notes: Tess, structure lens

Name: Tess · Home group: Dana’s group · Period __ · Meetings ___ & ___ · Section: “Kindred,” Chunk 3 · Expert lens: Structure

What a finished prep looks like. Kit section 6 / Organizer A completed for one reader, so students can compare their notes to a strong model. Paraphrase and short references only — no long copying. Paraphrase only — no quotations; verify details against the book.

Questions

Why must Dana keep a slaveholder alive?

What does her final injury mean?

3 · Important ideas

1. Dana travels to the past whenever Rufus’s life is threatened.

2. Each trip is longer and more dangerous than the last.

3. Her survival ties her to the plantation’s system of power.

Vocabulary

antebellum = before the Civil War.

patroller = one who enforced slavery by hunting the enslaved.

2 · Supporting details

1. Dana loses part of herself — literally — by the end (epilogue). → The past exacts a permanent cost. (Verify against the text.)

2. The compromises she makes to survive (middle sections). → Complicity forced by power. (Verify against the text.)

1 · Summary in my own words

A modern Black woman is repeatedly pulled into the antebellum South to keep her white ancestor alive, confronting slavery’s brutality and its hold on the present.

Bring to the circle

My interpretation: The past is not past; its power still shapes and scars the present.
Synthesis (two lenses): The time-travel structure carries the theme: Dana’s forced returns dramatize how slavery’s power reaches into the present. Text points (paraphrased): the epilogue’s cost and her mid-novel compromises.
After discussion: My thinking became clearer once the group compared evidence.

TEKS evidence: 6(C,D) supported inference; 7(A) theme through characters/events; 1(D) response to a peer. · Pair with the PlotPoint “Kindred” room.
