Text-specific discussion questions (open — more than one answer)

  • Predict: Four sisters vow to bear their “burdens” — which sister’s ambition will cost or reward her most?
  • Question: Why might Alcott frame the sisters’ growth as a “pilgrim’s progress”?
  • Clarify: What does Jo mean by wanting to “do something splendid”?
  • Summarize: Trace one sister’s main change in Part 1, without opinion.
  • Interpret the theme: How do the sisters’ choices reveal a theme about ambition versus duty? Use two details.
Optional Jigsaw lenses (p. 4): Character (Jo and her sisters) · Theme (ambition vs. duty) · Structure (the Pilgrim’s Progress frame) · Setting (the Civil War home front) · Language (the narrator’s moral asides).

Sample 3–2–1 notes: Beth, character (jo) lens

Name: Beth · Home group: Jo’s group · Period __ · Meetings ___ & ___ · Section: “Little Women,” Chunk 1 · Expert lens: Character (Jo)

What a finished prep looks like. Kit section 6 / Organizer A completed for one reader, so students can compare their notes to a strong model.

Questions

Which matters more to Jo — ambition or family?

Is sacrifice rewarded in Part 1?

3 · Important ideas

1. Jo wants independence and to become a writer.

2. She sacrifices for family — selling her hair to fund Marmee’s trip.

3. Beth’s illness draws the sisters closer together.

Vocabulary

genteel = respectably refined.

penitent = sorry for wrongdoing.

2 · Supporting details

1. Jo cuts and sells her hair (Part 1). → Shows sacrifice over vanity.

2. The sisters give their Christmas breakfast to the Hummels (opening). → Duty over comfort.

1 · Summary in my own words

Four sisters grow up in wartime poverty, each balancing personal ambition with love and duty to family.

Bring to the circle

My interpretation: Growing up means balancing one’s own dreams with care for others.
Synthesis (two lenses): Jo’s choices (character) carry the theme: her ambition matters, but her sacrifices define her. Text points: the hair (Chunk 1) and the Hummel breakfast (Chunk 1).
After discussion: My thinking became clearer once the group compared evidence.

TEKS evidence: 6(C,D) supported inference; 7(A) theme through characters/events; 1(D) response to a peer.
