Text-specific discussion questions (open — more than one answer)

  • Predict: After Della counts $1.87 the day before Christmas, what will she risk to give Jim a worthy gift? What clue supports your guess?
  • Question: Why might the narrator step in to compare Della and Jim to the magi? How does that change how we judge their choices?
  • Clarify: What does the narrator mean that they “most unwisely sacrificed the greatest treasures”? Is it really unwise?
  • Summarize: Each sold the one thing the other’s gift was meant for — restate that irony in order, without opinion.
  • Interpret the theme: How do their choices reveal what the story says about love versus money? Use two details.
Optional Jigsaw lenses (p. 4): Character (Della’s and Jim’s choices) · Plot (the parallel sacrifices, twist ending) · Language (the intruding narrator) · Theme (value isn’t material) · Point of view (the narrator’s direct address (5th reader)).

What a finished prep looks like. Kit section 6 / Organizer A completed for one reader, so students can compare their notes to a strong model.

Questions

Is a gift that can’t be used still the best gift?

Why call them the “wisest” right after calling the trade unwise?

3 · Important ideas

1. Jim sells his gold watch — his most prized possession — to buy Della combs.

2. Della had already cut and sold her long hair to buy Jim a chain for that watch.

3. Neither can use the gift they receive, yet both are moved, not angry.

Vocabulary

coveted = wanted badly for a long time.

the magi = the wise gift-bringers of the Christmas story.

2 · Supporting details

1. When Jim sees Della’s short hair he goes still — shock, not anger (the reveal). → His love isn’t about her appearance.

2. The combs Della had “coveted” are now useless without her hair (the reveal). → The gift’s worth is in the sacrifice.

1 · Summary in my own words

Both Della and Jim secretly sold their most treasured thing to buy the other a Christmas gift. The gifts can’t even be used — but the love and sacrifice behind them is the real point.

Bring to the circle

My interpretation: These details suggest the story measures love by what people give up, not by what they spend.
Synthesis (two lenses): The parallel sacrifices (plot) create the irony that proves the theme: value lives in the sacrifice, not the object. Text points: Della’s haircut in Chunk 1 and Jim’s sold watch in Chunk 2.
After discussion: My thinking became clearer once the group compared evidence.
