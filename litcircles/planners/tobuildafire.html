Text-specific discussion questions (open — more than one answer)

  • Predict: After the man ignores the old-timer’s advice, what will the cold force him to face? What clue supports it?
  • Question: Why might London give the dog instincts the man lacks? What does that contrast argue?
  • Clarify: What does “the trouble with him was that he was without imagination” mean for his fate?
  • Summarize: Order the man’s attempts to survive after he gets wet, without opinion.
  • Interpret the theme: How do the man’s choices and the setting reveal a theme about nature vs. pride? Use two details.
Optional Jigsaw lenses (p. 4): Character (the man and the dog) · Setting (the Yukon cold as antagonist) · Plot (the chain of small errors) · Theme (instinct vs. arrogance) · Language (repetition of temperature and warnings).

Sample 3–2–1 notes: Ravi, setting lens

Name: Ravi · Home group: Noah’s group · Period __ · Meetings ___ & ___ · Section: “To Build a Fire,” Chunk 2 · Expert lens: Setting

What a finished prep looks like. Kit section 6 / Organizer A completed for one reader, so students can compare their notes to a strong model.

Questions

Could the man have survived with the dog’s instincts?

Is the ending fate, or his own doing?

3 · Important ideas

1. The cold is an active force, not just a backdrop.

2. Small mistakes — getting wet, a badly placed fire — turn fatal.

3. The dog reads danger the man dismisses.

Vocabulary

conjectural = based on guessing.

apprehension = uneasy awareness of danger.

2 · Supporting details

1. The man’s spittle crackles in the air (early). → Shows how extreme and deadly the cold is.

2. Snow from the spruce buries his fire (middle). → Nature punishes his haste.

1 · Summary in my own words

A man travels alone in deadly cold, ignores a warning, and dies after failing to keep a fire; the dog moves on.

Bring to the circle

My interpretation: Nature is indifferent; survival needs humility and instinct, not confidence.
Synthesis (two lenses): The environment (setting) enacts the theme: pride without imagination cannot survive an indifferent nature. Text points: the crackling cold (Chunk 1) and the buried fire (Chunk 2).
After discussion: My thinking became clearer once the group compared evidence.

TEKS evidence: 6(C,D) supported inference; 7(A) theme through characters/events; 1(D) response to a peer. · Pair with the PlotPoint “To Build a Fire” room.
