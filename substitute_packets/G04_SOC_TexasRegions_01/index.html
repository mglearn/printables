The Four Regions of Texas

Grade: 4 Subject: Social Studies (Texas studies) Time: ~45 min core + ~15 min extension Social Studies

Overview

This is a self-contained, no-technology substitute packet in which Grade 4 students learn about the four natural regions of Texas — Mountains and Basins, the Great Plains, the North Central Plains, and the Coastal Plains — and explore how each region's geography (its land, weather, and water) shapes how people live and work there. Working alone with a pencil, students read four short original region profiles, study a simple map and a comparison table, then find one way two regions are alike and two ways they differ, explain a geography cause-and-effect, sort facts from opinions, evaluate a claim, and write a short evidence-based comparison of two regions. Directions are short and the writing space is generous for younger readers. Prompts are neutral and factual.

Start (5 minutes) — Find It on the Map

A region is a part of a place where the land and weather are much the same.

  1. Look at the map (Figure 1, described below). Find the Mountains and Basins region and write which corner of Texas it is in.
  2. Name one way the land or weather might change how people work. (Guess in your own words.)

Build (10 minutes) — Read the region profiles

Mountains and Basins (written for this packet)

The Mountains and Basins region is in the far west corner of Texas. It has the tallest mountains in the state and wide, dry valleys called basins between them. It is the driest part of Texas and gets very little rain. Because water is hard to find, few people live here. Some people raise cattle on huge ranches, and there is some farming where water can be brought in. Texas's highest peak, Guadalupe Peak, is in this region. (Type: original region summary; region: Mountains and Basins, far west.)

Great Plains (written for this packet)

The Great Plains region covers the northwest and west-central part of Texas. Much of it is high, flat, open land, including the very flat top part called the High Plains, or Llano Estacado. The weather is dry, and there is lots of grass. This region is known for large cattle ranches and for growing crops like cotton and wheat. Wind and oil are also important, so you will see wind turbines and oil wells here. (Type: original region summary; region: Great Plains, northwest.)

North Central Plains (written for this packet)

The North Central Plains region sits in the north-central part of Texas, between the Great Plains to the west and the Coastal Plains to the east. It has rolling hills, prairies, and some woods. It gets more rain than the regions farther west. People here raise cattle, grow crops, and drill for oil and gas. The large city of Fort Worth grew up in this region as a center for cattle and railroads. (Type: original region summary; region: North Central Plains, north-central.)

Coastal Plains (written for this packet)

The Coastal Plains region covers the south and east part of Texas, all the way to the Gulf of Mexico. It is the largest region and where the most people live. The land is low and flat, and it gets the most rain, so the soil is good for farming. Along the warm coast there are busy ports, fishing, and oil found both on land and offshore. Many of Texas's biggest cities, like Houston and San Antonio, are in this region. (Type: original region summary; region: Coastal Plains, south and east.)

Apply (20 minutes) — Read the map & table

  1. Map reading: using Figure 1, which region is farthest west, and which region touches the Gulf of Mexico?
  2. Table reading: (a) which region has the tallest mountains? (b) which region gets the most rain? (c) which region is where the city of Fort Worth grew up?
  3. Compare: pick two regions; name one way they are alike, and two ways they are different.
  4. Cause and effect: pick one region and explain how its geography (land, weather, or water) affects how people work there (2–3 sentences).
  5. Fact (F) or opinion (O)? Mark each:
    • a) The Mountains and Basins region is the driest part of Texas.
    • b) The Coastal Plains is the best region in Texas.
    • c) The Great Plains region is known for large cattle ranches.
    • d) Mountains are prettier than plains.
  6. Evaluate a claim: a student says "The land and weather are the same in all four regions of Texas." Does the packet support it? Choose: (A) Yes — the packet says they are the same; (B) No — the packet shows dry mountains in the far west, dry flat plains, rolling hills, and a rainy low coast in the south and east; (C) The packet does not talk about land or weather. Then explain your choice with one detail from a profile or Table 1.

Explain (10 minutes) — Claim, Evidence, Reasoning

Question 9. Pick two regions. How is the work people do different in those two regions, and why? Write a claim naming your two regions and how the work differs, support it with two pieces of evidence from the profiles, map, or Table 1, and explain your reasoning (how geography helps cause the difference).

Sentence stems you may use: "In ______ people mostly ______, but in ______ people mostly ______."; "One piece of evidence is… (from a profile / the map / Table 1)."; "A second piece of evidence is…"; "This happens because the land and weather are…"

Close (5 minutes) — ACE

  • Articulate: in your own words, tell a friend what a region is.
  • Connect: name one place in this packet (the map, a profile, or Table 1) that shows the land changing how people work.
  • Extend: which Texas region do you live in or closest to — name one way the land or weather there changes how people work or play.

Continue (optional, ~15 minutes) — Early finisher

Which region fits the job? Match each worker to the region that fits best and tell why using the packet: (1) a rancher who raises cattle on very dry, high, flat grassland; (2) a dock worker who loads ships at a busy port near warm ocean water; (3) a mountain guide who leads hikers up the tallest peak in Texas. For each, name the region and give one reason from a profile, the map, or Table 1.

Turn in

Hand in the whole packet with your name, class, and date, with questions 1–9 and the ACE box answered. Include the optional matching if you did it.
