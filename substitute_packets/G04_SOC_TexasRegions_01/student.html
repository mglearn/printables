The Four Regions of Texas

Grade: 4 Subject: Social Studies (Texas studies) Time: ~45 min core + ~15 min extension Work mode: Independent · pencil only Social Studies

Start Here

This is a paper reading activity about Texas. You will read four short region profiles, look at a map, and answer with a pencil. You do not need a computer or a book — everything you need is in this packet. Work by yourself. Write in full sentences when a question asks you to explain. If a question is hard, skip it, keep going, and come back. You may read the passages quietly aloud to yourself.

Build Read the Region Profiles 10 min

Mountains and Basins (written for this packet)

The Mountains and Basins region is in the far west corner of Texas. It has the tallest mountains in the state and wide, dry valleys called basins between them. It is the driest part of Texas and gets very little rain. Because water is hard to find, few people live here. Some people raise cattle on huge ranches, and there is some farming where water can be brought in. Texas's highest peak, Guadalupe Peak, is in this region.

Type: original region summary · Region: Mountains and Basins (far west)

Great Plains (written for this packet)

The Great Plains region covers the northwest and west-central part of Texas. Much of it is high, flat, open land, including the very flat top part called the High Plains, or Llano Estacado. The weather is dry, and there is lots of grass. This region is known for large cattle ranches and for growing crops like cotton and wheat. Wind and oil are also important, so you will see wind turbines and oil wells here.

Type: original region summary · Region: Great Plains (northwest)

North Central Plains (written for this packet)

The North Central Plains region sits in the north-central part of Texas, between the Great Plains to the west and the Coastal Plains to the east. It has rolling hills, prairies, and some woods. It gets more rain than the regions farther west. People here raise cattle, grow crops, and drill for oil and gas. The large city of Fort Worth grew up in this region as a center for cattle and railroads.

Type: original region summary · Region: North Central Plains (north-central)

Coastal Plains (written for this packet)

The Coastal Plains region covers the south and east part of Texas, all the way to the Gulf of Mexico. It is the largest region and where the most people live. The land is low and flat, and it gets the most rain, so the soil is good for farming. Along the warm coast there are busy ports, fishing, and oil found both on land and offshore. Many of Texas's biggest cities, like Houston and San Antonio, are in this region.

Type: original region summary · Region: Coastal Plains (south and east)

Word Bank

region
a part of a place where the land and weather are much the same.
geography
the land, water, and weather of a place.
landform
a natural shape of the land, like a mountain, hill, or plain.
climate
the usual weather of a place over time.
plain
a large area of flat or gently rolling land.
basin
a low, dry valley between mountains or hills.
ranching
raising cattle or other animals on a large area of land.
port
a place by the water where ships load and unload goods.
fact
something you can check and show is true.
opinion
what a person feels or likes; not proven true.
