Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  Line up the place values (ones under ones, tens under tens). Add or subtract from the right, one column at a time. When you add and a column is 10 or more, carry (regroup) the extra ten to the next column. When you subtract and the top digit is too small, borrow a ten from the next column. Example: 367 + 158 = 525; 502 − 148 = 354.

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. Before you add or subtract, what do you line up in each number?
  2. Which side do you start adding or subtracting from — the left or the right?
  3. When you add and a column adds up to 10 or more, what do you do?
  4. When you subtract and the top digit is too small, what do you do?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Set up 367 + 158 in columns (ones, tens, hundreds). Draw an arrow to show which column you start with, and mark where you carry a ten.

Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. Solve 367 + 158. Show your work and tell where you carried.
  2. Solve 502 − 148. Show your work and tell where you borrowed.
  3. Solve 245 + 476. Then check your answer by subtracting one addend from your total.

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

A student wrote 63 + 29 = 812 by writing the two ones together and the two tens together. Explain what went wrong and give the correct answer.

Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain why lining up place values matters. Use the words ones, tens, and column.

C

Connect

Connect carrying and borrowing to trading money. How is carrying a ten like trading 10 pennies for a dime?

E

Extend

Write a subtraction problem with three digits that needs borrowing two times. Solve it and show each borrow.

Confidence check. Circle one after you finish: I still need the cue / I need it once in a while / I can explain this without it.

Teacher Key — Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. The place values (ones under ones, tens under tens, and so on).
  2. The right (start with the ones column).
  3. Carry (regroup) the extra ten into the next column to the left.
  4. Borrow a ten from the next column to the left.

Sketch It — look for

Digits lined up by place value, an arrow pointing to the ones column as the start, and a carried 1 above the tens column (7 + 8 = 15, so write 5 and carry 1).

Apply It

  1. 367 + 158 = 525 (carry from ones to tens, and from tens to hundreds).
  2. 502 − 148 = 354 (borrow across the zero in the tens place).
  3. 245 + 476 = 721; check: 721 − 476 = 245.

Reason from Evidence — look for

The student did not regroup. 3 + 9 = 12, so write 2 and carry 1 ten; 1 + 6 + 2 = 9 tens. The correct answer is 63 + 29 = 92.

ACE — Extend look-for

A valid three-digit subtraction with two borrows, correctly solved (for example, 403 − 158 = 245).

Suggested use. Run Page 1 with the cue card available, Page 2 with the cue mostly covered, and Page 3 as independent reflection. Let students uncover the cue to self-correct in a different color.
