Division

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  Division means sharing a total into equal groups. In dividend ÷ divisor = quotient, the dividend is the total, the divisor is the number of groups (or size of each group), and the quotient is how many are in each group. Example: 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Sometimes it does not split evenly and there is a remainder left over: 13 ÷ 4 = 3 R1 (3 in each group with 1 left over).

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. What does division do to a total?
  2. In dividend ÷ divisor = quotient, which number is the total you start with?
  3. What is 12 ÷ 3?
  4. What is a remainder?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw 12 dots shared equally into 3 groups. Circle each group and write how many are in each.

Division — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. You have 12 crayons to share equally among 3 friends. How many does each friend get? Write the number sentence.
  2. Solve 13 ÷ 4. Write the quotient and the remainder.
  3. Solve 20 ÷ 5. Then write a multiplication sentence that checks your answer.

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

A student says 13 ÷ 4 = 4 because they rounded up. Explain why 3 R1 is correct and what the remainder means here.

Division — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain what the dividend, divisor, and quotient are. Use the example 12 ÷ 3 = 4 in your answer.

C

Connect

Connect division to multiplication. How does knowing 3 × 4 = 12 help you solve 12 ÷ 3?

E

Extend

Write a real story problem for 17 ÷ 5 where the remainder matters. Solve it and tell what the remainder means.

Teacher Key — Division

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. It shares (splits) the total into equal groups.
  2. The dividend.
  3. 4.
  4. The amount left over when a total does not split into equal groups evenly.

Sketch It — look for

12 dots split into 3 equal circled groups of 4, with 4 written for each group (12 ÷ 3 = 4).

Apply It

  1. Each friend gets 4 crayons; 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
  2. 13 ÷ 4 = 3 R1 (3 in each group, 1 left over).
  3. 20 ÷ 5 = 4; check: 5 × 4 = 20.

Reason from Evidence — look for

4 groups of 4 would be 16, which is too many. 4 fits into 13 three whole times (4 × 3 = 12) with 1 left over, so the answer is 3 R1. The remainder 1 is the leftover that cannot make another full group.

ACE — Extend look-for

A sensible story for 17 ÷ 5 = 3 R2, with the remainder explained (for example, 3 full groups and 2 items left over, or needing a 4th group/car for the leftovers).
