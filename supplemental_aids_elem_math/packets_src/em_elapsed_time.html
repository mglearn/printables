Telling & Elapsed Time

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  On an analog clock, the short hand shows the hour and the long hand shows the minutes. Each number is 5 minutes apart, so you can read to the minute by counting by fives and then ones. To find elapsed time (how much time has passed), count up on a number line from the start time to the end time. From 3:15 to 4:00, count 3:15 → 3:30 (15 min) → 4:00 (30 min), which is 45 minutes in all. Jump to the next easy time, then add the jumps.

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. On an analog clock, which hand shows the minutes?
  2. How many minutes are between one number and the next on the clock?
  3. What does elapsed time tell you?
  4. How much time is elapsed from 3:15 to 4:00?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw a number line from 3:15 to 4:00. Show the jumps you would use to count up, and label how many minutes each jump is.

Telling & Elapsed Time — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. Recess starts at 10:20 and ends at 10:45. How many minutes long is recess? Show your counting.
  2. A movie starts at 2:30 and ends at 4:00. How much time passed?
  3. The bus leaves at 7:50 and arrives at 8:15. How many minutes is the ride?

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

A friend said the time from 3:15 to 4:00 is only 15 minutes because they just looked at the minute numbers. Use counting up on a number line to explain the real answer.

Telling & Elapsed Time — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain how to find elapsed time by counting up on a number line. Use a start and end time as your example.

C

Connect

Connect elapsed time to something in your day, such as class, practice, or a car ride. Tell the start time, the end time, and how long it lasted.

E

Extend

A game starts at 1:40 and lasts 50 minutes. What time does it end? Show how you counted up.

Confidence check. Circle one after you finish: I still need the cue / I need it once in a while / I can explain this without it.

Teacher Key — Telling & Elapsed Time

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. The long hand (minute hand).
  2. 5 minutes.
  3. How much time has passed from a start time to an end time.
  4. 45 minutes.

Sketch It — look for

A number line from 3:15 to 4:00 with jumps such as 3:15 → 3:30 (15 min) and 3:30 → 4:00 (30 min), totaling 45 minutes.

Apply It

  1. 25 minutes (10:20 → 10:45).
  2. 90 minutes, or 1 hour 30 minutes (2:30 → 4:00).
  3. 25 minutes (7:50 → 8:00 is 10 min, 8:00 → 8:15 is 15 min).

Reason from Evidence — look for

Counting up shows 3:15 → 3:30 is 15 minutes and 3:30 → 4:00 is 30 more, so 15 + 30 = 45 minutes, not 15.

ACE — Extend look-for

1:40 + 50 minutes = 2:30. Count 1:40 → 2:00 (20 min), then 2:00 → 2:30 (30 min); 20 + 30 = 50 minutes.
