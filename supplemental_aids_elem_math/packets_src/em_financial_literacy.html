Money & Financial Literacy

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  Coins: penny 1¢, nickel 5¢, dime 10¢, quarter 25¢; a dollar bill is 100¢. To find a total, add the biggest coins first. To make change, count up from the price to the amount paid. Saving means keeping money for later; spending means using it now. Income is money you get: money you earn (from work or chores) or money you get as a gift. A budget plans for needs (things you must have) before wants (things that are nice to have).

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. How many cents is a quarter worth?
  2. To make change, do you count up from the price or down from it?
  3. What is the difference between saving and spending?
  4. In a budget, do you plan for needs or wants first?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw coins that make exactly 40¢ using the fewest coins you can. Label each coin with its value.

Money & Financial Literacy — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. You have 2 quarters, 1 dime, and 3 pennies. How much money is that in all? Add the biggest coins first.
  2. A snack costs 65¢. You pay with a one-dollar bill (100¢). Count up from 65¢ to find your change.
  3. Tell whether each is a need or a want: water, a new toy, food, a video game. Sort them into two groups.

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

Sam earns $5 for raking leaves and gets $5 as a birthday gift, so he has $10. He wants a $12 toy. Should he spend now or save? Explain your plan using the words income, save, and spend.

Money & Financial Literacy — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain how to make change by counting up. Use the words price, paid, and difference.

C

Connect

Connect income to your own life. Name one way you could earn money and one gift you might receive. Tell which is earned income.

E

Extend

You have $8 to budget. Plan for one need and one want, and tell how much you would save. Show that your amounts add up to $8 or less.

Confidence check. Circle one after you finish: I still need the cue / I need it once in a while / I can explain this without it.

Teacher Key — Money & Financial Literacy

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. 25 cents.
  2. Count up from the price to the amount paid.
  3. Saving keeps money to use later; spending uses money now.
  4. Needs first, then wants.

Sketch It — look for

Fewest coins for 40¢ is 3 coins: one quarter (25¢) + one dime (10¢) + one nickel (5¢) = 40¢.

Apply It

  1. 63¢ (25 + 25 + 10 + 1 + 1 + 1).
  2. 35¢ change (65¢ → 75¢ → 100¢, so 10 + 25 = 35).
  3. Needs: water, food. Wants: a new toy, a video game.

Reason from Evidence — look for

His income is $10 ($5 earned plus $5 gift), which is less than $12, so he cannot buy it yet. He should save the $10 (and earn or save $2 more) instead of spending now.

ACE — Extend look-for

A budget names one need and one want, sets an amount to save, and the three amounts total $8 or less (for example: need $4, want $2, save $2 = $8).

Suggested use. Run Page 1 with the cue card available, Page 2 with the cue mostly covered, and Page 3 as independent reflection. Let students uncover the cue to self-correct in a different color.
