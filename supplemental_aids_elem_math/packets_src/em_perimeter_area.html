Perimeter & Area

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  Perimeter is the distance all the way around a shape. To find it, add the lengths of all the sides. A rectangle that is 5 units long and 3 units wide has a perimeter of 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units. Area is the number of square units that cover the inside. For a rectangle, area = length × width. That same 5 × 3 rectangle has an area of 15 square units. Perimeter is measured in units; area is measured in square units.

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. What does perimeter measure?
  2. How do you find the area of a rectangle?
  3. What is the perimeter of a rectangle that is 5 units by 3 units?
  4. Is area measured in units or square units?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw a rectangle that is 5 units long and 3 units wide. Label each side. Then shade the inside and write the area in square units.

Perimeter & Area — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. A rectangle is 6 units long and 4 units wide. Find its perimeter. Show how you added the sides.
  2. A rectangle is 6 units long and 4 units wide. Find its area. Show the multiplication.
  3. A square garden has sides of 5 units. What is its perimeter and its area?

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

A friend measured a rectangle and wrote “area = 16 units.” Explain what is wrong with the label, and tell the correct unit for area.

Teacher Key — Perimeter & Area

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. The distance all the way around a shape (add all the sides).
  2. Multiply length × width.
  3. 16 units (5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16).
  4. Square units.

Sketch It — look for

A rectangle labeled 5 units and 3 units on its sides, inside shaded, with area written as 15 square units.

Apply It

  1. Perimeter = 6 + 4 + 6 + 4 = 20 units.
  2. Area = 6 × 4 = 24 square units.
  3. Perimeter = 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 units; area = 5 × 5 = 25 square units.

Reason from Evidence — look for

Area cannot be labeled in plain units; it counts the squares inside, so it must be labeled in square units. (A value of 16 sounds like a perimeter, not an area.)

ACE — Extend look-for

Any two of: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, or 3 × 4 — each gives an area of 12 square units.
