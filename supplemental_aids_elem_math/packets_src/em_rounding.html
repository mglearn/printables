Rounding Numbers

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  First find the rounding place (the place you are rounding to). Then look at the digit just to its right. If that digit is 5 or more, round up; if it is 4 or less, stay the same. All digits after the rounding place become 0. Example: round 47 to the nearest ten. The tens digit is 4; the digit to the right is 7 (5 or more), so round up to 50.

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. After you find the rounding place, which digit do you look at?
  2. If the digit to the right is 5 or more, what do you do?
  3. If the digit to the right is 4 or less, what do you do?
  4. Round 47 to the nearest ten.

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw a number line from 40 to 50. Mark 47. Show whether 47 is closer to 40 or 50, and label where it rounds.

Rounding Numbers — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Idea

  1. Round 384 to the nearest hundred. Which digit did you look at?
  2. Round 2,650 to the nearest hundred. Explain your choice.
  3. Round 6.28 to the nearest tenth. Which digit did you look at?

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

Leo rounds 149 to the nearest hundred and gets 200. Is he correct? Use the rounding rule and the digit to the right to explain the right answer.

Rounding Numbers — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain the steps for rounding a number. Use the words rounding place, digit to the right, and zero.

C

Connect

Connect rounding to real life. Tell how rounding could help you estimate the cost of items at a store.

E

Extend

Write a number that rounds to 500 when rounded to the nearest hundred but is not 500. Explain why it works.

Confidence check. Circle one after you finish: I still need the cue / I need it once in a while / I can explain this without it.

Teacher Key — Rounding Numbers

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. The digit just to the right of the rounding place.
  2. Round up (add 1 to the rounding place).
  3. Keep the rounding-place digit the same.
  4. 50.

Sketch It — look for

Number line 40 to 50 with 47 marked between 45 and 50. 47 is closer to 50, so it rounds to 50.

Apply It

  1. 400. The digit to the right (the tens digit 8) is 5 or more, so round up.
  2. 2,700. The tens digit is 5, so round the hundreds place up from 6 to 7.
  3. 6.3. The digit to the right (the hundredths 8) is 5 or more, so the tenths round up.

Reason from Evidence — look for

No. To round 149 to the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit 4, which is 4 or less, so the hundreds stay the same. 149 rounds to 100.

ACE — Extend look-for

Any number from 450 to 549 (except 500), such as 480 or 512, rounds to 500 because the tens digit decides whether the hundreds place stays 5.

Suggested use. Run Page 1 with the cue card available, Page 2 with the cue mostly covered, and Page 3 as independent reflection. Let students uncover the cue to self-correct in a different color.
