Weather & Weather Tools

Use the cue, then fade the cue. Study the cue card for 60 seconds. Then cover it before answering Round 1. Check the card only after you commit to an answer.

Cue focus  Match the tool to the job: a thermometer measures temperature, a rain gauge measures precipitation, a wind vane shows wind direction, an anemometer measures wind speed, and a barometer measures air pressure. Weather is what happens day to day. Climate is the usual weather pattern over a long time.

Round 1 — Retrieve It

  1. Which tool measures how hot or cold the air is?
  2. Which tool measures how much rain has fallen?
  3. Which tool measures how fast the wind is blowing?
  4. What is the difference between weather and climate?

Round 2 — Sketch It

Draw a wind vane and an anemometer. Label which one shows wind direction and which one measures wind speed.

Weather & Weather Tools — Apply It

Keep the cue card covered. Answer first. Then use the cue to check, revise, and explain what you changed.

Round 3 — Apply the Tool

  1. You want to know if today is warmer than yesterday. Which tool would you use, and what unit does it measure?
  2. A pilot needs to know which way the wind is coming from. Which tool would help, and what does it show?
  3. You want to record how much it rained during a storm. Which tool would you use, and how does it work?

Round 4 — Reason from Evidence

A student says, "It snowed here today, so this place has a cold climate." Is that good reasoning? Use the difference between weather and climate to explain.

Weather & Weather Tools — ACE It

Use the cue card only if you need it. The goal is to show what you can explain, connect, and use on your own.

A

Articulate

Explain why weather forecasters use many tools instead of just looking at the sky. Give one example of a tool and its job.

C

Connect

Connect a barometer reading to a change in weather. Air pressure often drops before a storm—how could that help someone plan their day?

E

Extend

Design a simple weather station for your school. List every tool you would include and what each one measures.

Confidence check. Circle one after you finish: I still need the cue / I need it once in a while / I can explain this without it.

Teacher Key — Weather & Weather Tools

Remove this page before student copying. Answers below give expected ideas, not required wording.

Retrieve It

  1. A thermometer (measures temperature).
  2. A rain gauge (measures precipitation).
  3. An anemometer (measures wind speed).
  4. Weather is what happens day to day; climate is the usual weather pattern in a place over a long time.

Sketch It — look for

A wind vane labeled as showing wind direction (arrow that turns to point where wind comes from) and an anemometer labeled as measuring wind speed (spinning cups).

Apply It

  1. A thermometer, measured in degrees (°F or °C).
  2. A wind vane; it turns to show the direction the wind is coming from.
  3. A rain gauge; it collects rain so you can measure how deep the water is.

Reason from Evidence — look for

Not good reasoning. One snowy day is weather. Climate is the pattern over many years. You would need many years of records to describe the climate.

ACE — Extend look-for

Tools listed match their jobs: thermometer→temperature, rain gauge→precipitation, wind vane→wind direction, anemometer→wind speed, barometer→air pressure.
